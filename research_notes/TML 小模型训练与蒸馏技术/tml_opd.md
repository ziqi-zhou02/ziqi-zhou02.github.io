## What exactly is the algorithm in TML's original OPD post (loss, sampling, RL-style implementation, discount, relation to SFT/RL, credited prior work)?

### Takeaway
TML's OPD is deliberately minimal: sample rollouts from the student exactly as in RL, score the *sampled* tokens with the teacher via one `compute_logprobs` forward pass, set the per-token advantage to the negative sampled-token reverse KL (`teacher_logprob − student_logprob`), use discount factor 0, give zero environment reward, and train with the ordinary importance-sampling policy-gradient loss. It is "a one-line change" to an RL trainer with KL regularisation (swap the regulariser model for the teacher); it uses no teacher sampling, no full logits/top-k, and no reward model, and it credits DAgger, process reward models, Agarwal et al. (GKD), Gu et al. (MiniLLM) and the Qwen3 technical report.

### Cited Findings

Framing (all 2025-10-27, [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/))
- Three training stages: pre-training (general capacities), mid-training (domain knowledge), post-training (targeted behaviour). OPD is positioned as a post-training method applied "on models that have a foundation of capabilities from pre- and mid-training."
- The 2x2 the post is built on: SFT = off-policy sampling / dense reward; RL = on-policy / sparse; OPD = on-policy / dense.
- Against RL: RL "provides very sparse feedback, teaching a fixed number of bits per training episode regardless of the number of tokens used."
- Against off-policy distillation/SFT: "the student learns in contexts frequented by the teachers, not ones the student itself will often find itself in. This can cause compounding error"; also the student "can learn to imitate the teacher's style and confidence but not necessarily its factual accuracy" (citing Gudibande et al. 2023).
- Off-policy distillation as TML defines it uses sampled teacher sequences, not logits: "We can use the teacher's full next-token distribution at each step (often called 'logit distillation') or just sample given sequences. In practice, sampling sequences provides an unbiased estimation of the teacher's distribution and arrives at the same objective."
- Core idea, verbatim: "sample trajectories from the *student* model and use a high-performing teacher to grade *each token* of each trajectory."

Loss (verbatim formula from the post)
- "For simplicity, we choose the per-token reverse KL—the divergence between the student's (π_θ) and teacher's (π_teacher) distribution for each token conditioned on the same prior trajectory":
  `KL(π_θ || π_teacher) = E_{x∼π_θ} [ log π_θ(x_{t+1} | x_{1..t}) − log π_teacher(x_{t+1} | x_{1..t}) ]` — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Discount: "For simplicity, we use a discount factor of zero: at any given timestep, the student only optimizes the immediate next token, with no consideration for future tokens." Footnote: "Although more mathematically correct, we do not find discount factors > 0 to improve performance in practice, so we choose zero for simplicity." The cookbook README repeats this: "You can optionally increase `kl_discount_factor` to optimize discounted future KL, but we generally do not observe this to improve performance." — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/); [cookbook distillation README](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/tree/main/tinker_cookbook/recipes/distillation)
- Why reverse KL: it "has natural synergy with RL, which generally optimizes a form of sequence-level reverse KL induced by the reward model"; it is "'unhackable' in the sense that low KL always corresponds to a high probability of desirable behavior from the teacher model's point of view"; it is "mode seeking"; and it "reduces exposure bias."
- Other losses are acknowledged but not explored: "See Agarwal et al. for an analysis of various loss function choices."
- Sampled tokens, not logits: "In this post, we do not consider logit (top-k) distillation in any of our experiments, which could be used to further improve compute efficiency."

Procedure / pseudocode (verbatim from the post)
- Four steps: (1) "Initialize teacher client… We use a sampling client, as we do not need to propagate logprobs through the teacher model." (2) "Sample trajectories. We sample rollouts from the student exactly as we would in RL. During sampling, RL already computes the student's logprobs." (3) "Compute reward. We query the teacher client with `compute_logprobs` on the sampled trajectories, which returns the teacher's logprobs … on the tokens x sampled by the student." (4) "Train with RL. We set the per-token advantage to the negative reverse KL, and call the RL importance-sampling loss function."
- Pseudocode in the post:
  ```
  teacher_client = service_client.create_sampling_client(base_model=teacher_config.base_model, model_path=teacher_config.load_checkpoint_path)
  trajectories = do_group_rollout(student_client, env_group_builder)
  sampled_logprobs = trajectories.loss_fn_inputs["logprobs"]
  teacher_logprobs = teacher_client.compute_logprobs(trajectories)
  reverse_kl = sampled_logprobs - teacher_logprobs
  trajectories["advantages"] = -reverse_kl
  training_client.forward_backward(trajectories, loss_fn="importance_sampling")
  ```
- "Our implementation can actually be a one-line change on top of RL implementations which use KL regularization: we merely swap out the regularizer model."
- Compute properties claimed: "Since it doesn't require a rollout to finish sampling to calculate the reward, we can use shorter or partial rollouts for training. Querying the teacher's log probabilities also requires just a single forward pass from the larger model, while the trajectories are generated by the smaller and cheaper student." "We do not require a separate reward or labeling model either."
- Why an SFT/mid-training initialisation is needed: "SFT, using forwards KL, adds support for new tokens. Reverse-KL methods can then perform mode seeking within the initialization's support."

The importance-sampling loss the advantage is fed into
- Tinker defines `L_IS(θ) = E_{x∼q}[ (p_θ(x)/q(x)) · A(x) ]`, implemented as `prob_ratio = torch.exp(target_logprobs - sampling_logprobs); loss = -(prob_ratio * advantages).sum()`, with per-token inputs `target_tokens`, `logprobs` (sampler's), `advantages` — [Tinker docs: importance sampling](https://tinker-docs.thinkingmachines.ai/tinker/losses/importance-sampling/)

How OPD differs from RL and from process supervision, in TML's words
- "Reinforcement learning and on-policy distillation both learn via reverse KL, pruning the space of actions present in the base policy. The difference is in the density of reward… reinforcement learning only teaches O(1) bits per episode. In contrast, distillation teaches O(N) bits per episode, where N is the number of tokens."
- "RL searches in the space of semantic strategies… Once a good strategy is found, distillation serves as a shortcut for learning it: on-policy distillation does not need to model the intermediate strategies during the curriculum of RL, but rather only the final strategy learned."
- "In essence, we have treated the language model itself as a reward model, with high-probability behaviors being rewarded" (connected to DPO and inverse RL). "Any instruction-tuned open-weight model can be used as a reward model in this sense; we merely need access to the `compute_logprobs` function."
- Illustration: Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 student graded by Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507 on a SimpleBench item; the teacher "penalizes tokens that start phrases which lead the student astray, intuitively corresponding to important 'forking tokens'", while "the final answer, though wrong, isn't penalized—it is entirely predictable conditional on the whole preceding sequence."
- All from [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)

Credited prior work (verbatim attribution)
- "Our work with on-policy distillation draws inspiration from DAGGER (Ross et al, 2010), an iterative SFT algorithm that includes teacher evaluations of student-visited states. It is also similar to process reward modeling (Lightman et al, 2023)… We extend prior on-policy distillation work by Agarwal et al [On-Policy Distillation of Language Models: Learning from Self-Generated Mistakes, 2023], Gu et al [MiniLLM, 2023], and the Qwen3 team [Qwen3 Technical Report, 2025]. Using the Tinker training API, we replicate Qwen3's result of achieving equivalent performance on reasoning benchmarks with on-policy distillation for a fraction the cost of RL."
- Also cited: Bengio et al. 2015 (scheduled sampling / exposure bias), Wang et al. 2025 ("forking tokens"), Cobbe et al. 2020 (Phasic Policy Gradient, for alternating fine-tune/distill phases), Rafailov et al. 2023 (DPO), Ng & Russell 2000 (inverse RL), Chen et al. 2025 ("Retaining by Doing"), Shenfeld et al. 2025 ("RL's Razor"), and DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp for "specialist distillation": "on-policy learning can be a crucial tool for augmenting similar distillation-based 'model merging' setups."
- All from [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)

### Inferences
- The objective is a single-sample Monte-Carlo estimate of per-token reverse KL: the teacher is evaluated only at the token the student sampled, so the estimator is unbiased but high-variance, and the gradient is the REINFORCE/score-function form `∇ = E[(p_θ/q)·∇log p_θ(x_t) · (log π_teacher(x_t) − log q(x_t))]` with the advantage treated as a constant (it is computed from sampler log-probs, with no gradient through it). This is why TML frames logit/top-k distillation as a compute-efficiency upgrade rather than a different method.
- With γ = 0 the method is, in effect, per-token greedy imitation at student-visited states (DAgger with a soft teacher label observed at one sampled token); it does not account for the effect of token t on later-state KL. TML calls γ > 0 "more mathematically correct" but empirically unnecessary.
- The post gives no advantage baseline, normalisation or clipping; the code (next section) confirms none is applied.

### Gaps
- The post never states the sampling temperature, rollout length, learning rate or optimiser used for its headline curves; these are only recoverable from the cookbook (next section), and the cookbook defaults are not guaranteed to equal the blog-run settings (especially for the full-fine-tuning curves, which cannot be run on public Tinker LoRA).
- No analysis of estimator variance, of teacher calibration on student-visited (off-teacher-distribution) states, or of alternative divergences is given; TML defers to Agarwal et al.
- Kevin Lu's own X thread on the post was not retrievable.

## What experiments and numbers did TML report (reasoning distillation, compute comparison, personalization/continual learning, data reuse)?

### Takeaway
Two experiments carry the post: (1) math reasoning, where starting from a 400K-prompt SFT checkpoint of Qwen3-8B-Base (60% AIME'24), OPD reaches 70% in about 150 steps (~77K prompts, 4 samples each) at a claimed 9–30x lower FLOP cost than extrapolated SFT-2M; and (2) personalization, where OPD from the original Qwen3-8B on Tulu3 prompts restores IF-eval from 79% to 83% (original 85%) after a document mid-train, while internal-QA knowledge rises to 41%. A discussion-section self-distillation experiment adds the 7–10x-fewer-steps / 50–100x-compute claim versus RL, and a single-prompt experiment shows OPD tolerates heavy prompt reuse. The widely repeated "1,800 vs 17,920 GPU hours" figures are Qwen3's numbers quoted by TML, not TML's own run.

### Cited Findings

Reasoning experiment setup (2025-10-27, [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/))
- Student Qwen3-8B-Base; nominal teacher Qwen3-32B. Footnote: "We actually use Qwen3-8B as a teacher, as it performs slightly better. However, for the purpose of comparing compute, we can still measure the FLOPs of the 32B model."
- Stage 1 (off-policy distillation = SFT) on OpenThoughts-3 (prompts/responses generated by QwQ-32B): "Training the student (Qwen3-8B-Base) on 400k prompts with full fine-tuning achieves a score of 60% on AIME'24." SFT scaling is "log-linear"; caption: "After the initial 50-100K prompts, performance follows a predictable log-linear scaling curve… we observe worse LoRA performance when running large-scale SFT with high batch size."
- SFT extrapolation: "we estimate that the model would achieve 70% on AIME'24 at approximately 2M prompts. This extrapolation requires the scaling law to hold up without stalling, which isn't trivial." Reference points given: DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B gets 86% (prompt count unspecified); Qwen2.5-7B / Qwen2.5-14B get 55.5% / 69.7% after 800k DeepSeek-R1 distillation prompts.
- RL reference is not TML's own run: "The Qwen3 technical report reaches performance of 67.6% on the benchmark using 17,920 GPU hours of RL on top of a similar SFT initialization. It is hard to compare this directly to the cost of distillation, but given some reasonable assumptions about the SFT training stack, this is similar to the cost of training on 2M off-policy distillation prompts."
- Qwen3 table reproduced by TML ("From Qwen3 Technical Report, Table 21"): Off-policy distillation 55.0% AIME'24 / 55.6% GPQA-Diamond / GPU hours unreported; + Reinforcement learning 67.6% / 61.3% / 17,920; + On-policy distillation 74.4% / 63.3% / 1,800. "The Qwen team also reports reaching a higher score of 74.4 on AIME'24 at one-tenth the cost of RL with on-policy distillation, which served as inspiration for our work."
- TML's OPD result: "Starting from the 400k SFT checkpoint, on-policy distillation achieves an AIME'24 of 70% in about 150 steps." Footnote: "150 steps corresponds to about 77K prompts; we train with 4 samples per prompt."
- LoRA vs full fine-tuning (figure caption): "On-policy distillation is significantly more compute-efficient than SFT, especially for LoRA models. At rank = 32, LoRA trails full finetuning by 13% after SFT, but only 6% after on-policy distillation."

Compute table (verbatim values; CE = compute efficiency vs SFT-2M)

| Method | AIME'24 | Teacher FLOPs | Student FLOPs | CE vs SFT-2M |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Initialization: SFT-400K | 60% | 8.5 × 10^20 | 3.8 × 10^20 | – |
| SFT-2M (extrapolated) | ~70% (extrapolated) | 3.4 × 10^21 | 1.5 × 10^21 | 1× |
| Reinforcement learning | 68% | - | - | ≈1× |
| On-policy distillation | 70% | 8.4 × 10^19 | 8.2 × 10^19 | 9-30× |

- "We find a baseline cost reduction of 9x when the SFT dataset is given, as in our example with OpenThoughts-3, or is amortized across many training runs." Footnote: "CE = (Student + Teacher) / (Student)" — i.e. teacher FLOPs are not counted for off-policy but are for on-policy. "Since this computation can be cheaply parallelized across GPUs, the cost reduction in GPU hours is closer to 18x."
- "If we include the full cost of the teacher model in off-policy distillation – ie, including the additional cost of sampling from the teacher model – the total cost reduction is about 30x." Footnote: "CE = (Student + Teacher) / (Student + Teacher)".
- Caveat stated with the table: FLOPs "penalizes methods that can be effectively parallelized on GPUs. In particular, it overestimates the practical cost of computing log-probs."
- All from [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)

Personalization / "internal assistant" experiment (same post)
- Goal: an assistant with (a) knowledge of internal company documents, measured by an "internal QA" eval, and (b) instruction following, measured by IF-eval. Start from post-trained Qwen3-8B (not the base model).
- Baseline against forgetting: Tulu3 prompts re-sampled with Qwen3-8B as "background" chat data ("acts as a forwards KL regularizer"); "sampling from Qwen3-8B is better than Qwen3-32B for preserving chat capabilities."
- Finding: "although mixing in at least 30% of chat data helps preserve most instruction-following ability, there is no weighting which maintains the original performance on IF-eval." Footnote: "This is true even if the SFT dataset contains 100% chat data." IF-eval degrades during mid-training for every mix; with a linear LR schedule "the degradation eventually flattens and slowly begins to recover… However, performance never fully recovers." LoRA "learns less (knowledge) and still forgets its original post-training behaviors."
- Fix: "we run on-policy distillation with the earlier version of the model, Qwen3-8B, as the teacher on Tulu3 prompts. Note that this phase of training has no relation to the internal document data, and is designed solely to recover instruction following."
- Results table (verbatim):

| Model | Internal QA Eval (Knowledge) | IF-eval (Chat) |
|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-8B | 18% | 85% |
| + midtrain (100%) | 43% | 45% |
| + midtrain (70%) | 36% | 79% |
| + midtrain (70%) + distill | 41% | 83% |

- "on-policy distillation recovers nearly full performance on IF-eval without losing any knowledge; we also observe some positive transfer between chat capabilities and the model's 'knowledge' performance on the internal QA eval."
- Speed (cookbook, not the post): "we saw IF-eval recover within approximately 100 steps" — [cookbook distillation README](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/tree/main/tinker_cookbook/recipes/distillation)
- Proposed continual-learning loop: "We could alternate between phases of fine-tuning on new data and distillation to recover behavior" (citing Phasic Policy Gradient).

Discussion-section experiments (same post)
- RL-vs-OPD head-to-head ("self-distillation" of an RL policy): (1) start with Qwen3-8B-Base, no SFT; (2) run RL on DeepMath "matching our procedure from LoRA Without Regret. We use a LoRA rank of 128"; that model is the teacher; (3) OPD from it back into the base model. Result: "distillation reaches the teacher's level of performance approximately 7-10x faster than RL with matched model architecture (LoRA rank 128). The reverse KL decreases to near-zero and the AIME score is recovered in under 10 gradient steps, while RL took 70 steps to reach that level." "Cumulatively, the reduction in compute required is on the order of 50-100x."
- Why 7–10x steps becomes 50–100x compute: (a) "While RL requires training at approximately the evaluation context (in order for the policy to learn the context limit and not incur format penalty), distillation learns reasonably at shorter context lengths, as there is no sharp cutoff in reward"; (b) "When the SFT initialization is strong, on-policy distillation works effectively with much smaller batch sizes since it provides significantly more bits per episode, thereby reducing gradient noise." Footnote on (b): "ie: when the teacher policy is within the support of the student policy. When this is not true, as in 'Distillation for reasoning', we require a significantly larger batch size."
- Data reuse / multi-epoch: "With RL, training multiple epochs on the same prompt often leads to simple memorization of the final answer." Experiment: train Qwen3-8B-Base on a single randomly chosen DeepMath prompt ("Evaluate the limit: lim_{x→∞} √x (∛(x+1) − ∛(x−1))") "for 20 consecutive steps, each with a batch of 256 rollouts, for 5120 graded sequences in total… we do approximately match the performance of the teacher model despite only training on a single prompt." Caption: "Our default configuration (also used in the personalization experiments) runs on-policy distillation with 64 prompts / batch, and 4 samples / prompt. All methods shown are trained with 256 samples / batch."
- SFT on own samples drifts: a Tulu3-prompt dataset sampled from Qwen3-32B at temperature 1.0 ("has a KL of zero against Qwen3-32B"); "any practical learning rate that's greater than zero leads to a degradation of performance on the instruction-following evaluation!" Explanation offered: "every finite batch will exhibit a slightly different distribution in practice… This process turns training on one's own samples into off-policy training over time." Contrast: "On-policy distillation always stays on-policy, and since the teacher stays fixed, the student converges on the teacher's desirable behavior, without regressing in the self-distillation setting as SFT does."

Cookbook-reported reproduction numbers (LoRA on Tinker; these differ from the blog's full-fine-tuning numbers)
- Original README (2025-10-27, Qwen3-8B-Base): SFT "~55% using a rank-128 LoRA after 3000 steps"; OPD "~65% using a rank-128 LoRA after 100 steps" — [tinker-cookbook commit 330b73d](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/commit/330b73d8d763b0f65bcf04e643cc6f082e002554)
- Rerun README (2026-06-15, Qwen3.5-9B-Base student, Qwen3.5-9B teacher): SFT "~65% using a rank-128 LoRA after 3000 steps"; OPD "~76.7% using a rank-128 LoRA after 200 steps with 16k-token rollouts"; "The AIME'24 scores above were evaluated with `temperature=1.0`, `top_p=1.0`, `top_k=-1`, and `max_tokens=64000`." — [tinker-cookbook commit ca232b0](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/commit/ca232b084411029df565a36095642918a097fa7f); [cookbook distillation README](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/tree/main/tinker_cookbook/recipes/distillation)

### Inferences
- The CE arithmetic checks out from the table: OPD total = 8.4e19 + 8.2e19 = 1.66e20; SFT-2M student-only 1.5e21 / 1.66e20 ≈ 9.0x; SFT-2M student+teacher (3.4e21 + 1.5e21) / 1.66e20 ≈ 29.5x; and 1.5e21 / 8.2e19 (student FLOPs only) ≈ 18x, matching the "closer to 18x" GPU-hour remark. The SFT-2M row is exactly 4x the SFT-400K row on both columns (8.5e20 x 4 = 3.4e21; 3.8e20 x 4 = 1.5e21), so it is the *incremental* cost of the extra 1.6M prompts beyond the shared 400K checkpoint, which is the fair comparison.
- 77K prompts over 150 steps is ~512 prompts per step, i.e. ~2,048 rollouts per step with 4 samples per prompt, matching the README's `groups_per_batch=512` for DeepMath. The reasoning run therefore used an 8x larger batch than the personalization default (64 prompts x 4), consistent with the footnote that a larger batch is needed when the teacher is not within the student's support.
- The headline comparisons are weaker than they look: the SFT comparator is an extrapolation, the RL comparator is another lab's number on a "similar" initialisation, and the teacher whose FLOPs are counted (32B) is not the teacher actually used (Qwen3-8B, same size as the student). With an 8B teacher the real teacher FLOPs would be lower than tabulated, so the table is conservative on that axis; but the experiment is then closer to "recover a post-trained sibling's behaviour into its own base model" than "compress a 32B model into 8B".
- Secondary coverage (e.g. Maginative, cited in search results) attributes "1,800 GPU hours vs 17,920" to TML's experiment; in the primary source those are Qwen3 Table 21 figures. TML reports FLOPs, not GPU hours, for its own run.
- In the personalization result the mid-trained student (36% knowledge / 79% IF) ends at 41% / 83%; IF-eval is still 2 points below the original 85%, so "recovers nearly full performance" is accurate and "fully recovers" would not be.

### Gaps
- No GPQA or other out-of-domain evaluation for TML's own reasoning run; AIME'24 only (a 30-problem benchmark, so a 10-point gap is 3 problems; the post does not report seeds or confidence intervals in text).
- The internal-document corpus, the internal QA eval and the mid-training hyperparameters are not released; the personalization experiment is not reproducible end to end (the README says "you will have to provide your own SFT initialization").
- Exact numbers behind the figures (per-step AIME curves, KL curves, the document:chat ratio sweep) are only in images.
- The 50–100x figure is described qualitatively ("on the order of"); no table decomposes it into steps x context length x batch size.

## What is in the Tinker cookbook implementation (paths, recipe structure, hyperparameters, teacher/student pairs)?

### Takeaway
The implementation is ~520 lines in `tinker_cookbook/distillation/train_on_policy.py` plus four recipe scripts under `tinker_cookbook/recipes/distillation/`; it reuses the RL trainer's rollout, datum-assembly and `train_step` code and only adds `incorporate_kl_penalty`, which adds `−kl_penalty_coef · mask · (student_logprob − teacher_logprob)` to each token's advantage (uncentred, unnormalised), with zero environment reward. Defaults: LoRA rank 128, `group_size=4`, `groups_per_batch` 512 (DeepMath) or 64 (Tulu3), LR 1e-4 (LoRA) / 5e-5 (full FT), temperature 1.0, `max_tokens` 4096 (16384 in the 2026 rerun), `kl_discount_factor=0`, Adam β=(0.9, 0.95). Since March 2026 the same trainer also runs multi-turn tool-use episodes in Harbor sandboxes.

Core function (verbatim essentials of `incorporate_kl_penalty`) — [train_on_policy.py](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/blob/main/tinker_cookbook/distillation/train_on_policy.py)
- Docstring: "Compute reverse KL between the student (log p) and the teacher model (log q), computed as log p - log q. We then adjust the advantages in-place as the negative reverse KL."
- Teacher scoring: for each datum, `full_sequence = datum.model_input.append_int(target_tokens[-1])`; `teacher_logprobs = await teacher_client.compute_logprobs(full_sequence)`; then `reverse_kl = (sampled_logprobs − torch.tensor(teacher_logprobs[1:])) * mask`.
- Advantage: `kl_advantages = -kl_penalty_coef * mask * reverse_kl`; `if kl_discount_factor > 0: kl_advantages = discounted_future_sum_vectorized(kl_advantages, kl_discount_factor)`; `advantages += kl_advantages`.
- `discounted_future_sum_vectorized(x, γ)` computes, for position i, `sum_{k≥0} γ^k · x[i+k]` — [rl/metrics.py](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/blob/main/tinker_cookbook/rl/metrics.py)
- Logged metric: `teacher_kl` = sum of masked `(log p − log q)` over the batch / number of unmasked tokens (also per dataset as `teacher_kl/dataset_{i}`).
- In-code caveat: "Note: if your teacher has a different renderer than the student, you may want to modify the full_sequence_inputs_D to match the teacher's renderer."
- Base advantages come from `compute_advantages(trajectory_groups)`, which centres total reward within each group (`rewards_G − rewards_G.mean()`); with the zero-reward environment these are all zero, so the KL term is the entire advantage — [rl/data_processing.py](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/blob/main/tinker_cookbook/rl/data_processing.py)

Training loop
- `do_sync_training` is "fully synchronous on-policy training": each iteration (optionally evaluates), gets a batch of env-group builders, samples `group_size` rollouts per prompt from the current student sampling client at `temperature`/`max_tokens` (`do_remove_constant_reward_groups=False`), builds datums, adds the KL advantage, runs `train_step`, then saves a checkpoint to obtain a fresh sampling client for the next iteration — [train_on_policy.py](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/blob/main/tinker_cookbook/distillation/train_on_policy.py)
- `train_step` uses `tinker.AdamParams(learning_rate=learning_rate, beta1=0.9, beta2=0.95, eps=1e-8)`, splitting the batch into `num_substeps` forward_backward + optim_step pairs — [rl/train.py](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/blob/main/tinker_cookbook/rl/train.py)
- Student init: `create_lora_training_client(model_name, rank=lora_rank)` or weights from `load_checkpoint_path` (fresh optimiser). Teacher: `service_client.create_sampling_client(base_model=…, model_path=…)`, one per dataset.
- Environment: `PromptOnlyEnv` ("Environment that only provides prompts with no rewards"): `check_format` always True, `check_answer` always False, `step` returns `reward=0.0, episode_done=True`. Prompts: DeepMath-103K `question` field; Tulu3 "first user message from each conversation"; `max_prompt_tokens=1024` truncation — [datasets.py](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/blob/main/tinker_cookbook/distillation/datasets.py)
- Multi-teacher: `CompositeDataset` draws `groups_per_batch` groups from each dataset every step and concatenates them; each datum is routed to its dataset's teacher. README: "This can be used to run multi-teacher distillation, although we do not showcase this in our blog post."

### Inferences
- A minimal reimplementation outside Tinker: for each prompt sample K=4 completions from the student at T=1 recording per-token sampled log-probs `lq`; teacher-force prompt+completion through the teacher to get `lt` at each completion token; set `A_t = lt_t − lq_t` (no baseline, no normalisation, completion tokens only); loss = `−Σ_t exp(lp_t − lq_t) · A_t` where `lp_t` is the current student log-prob (ratio ≈ 1 with one update per batch); Adam(β1=0.9, β2=0.95, eps=1e-8), LR 1e-4 for rank-128 LoRA or 5e-5 for full fine-tuning; resample from the updated student every step.
- The OPD path differs from the cookbook's generic RL KL-penalty in one detail: the RL trainer's `incorporate_kl_penalty` adds `kl_penalty_coef · mask · (avg_logp_diff − logprob_diff)` (batch-mean-centred) against a `KLReferenceConfig` model (configurable since 2026-01-27, PR #326), while the OPD version uses the uncentred `−(log p − log q)`. So "swap the regulariser model" is literally available in the RL trainer (centred variant, combinable with task reward), and the dedicated OPD trainer is the uncentred, reward-free variant. The uncentred advantage is negative in expectation (its mean is −KL), i.e. there is no baseline; TML does not discuss this choice.
- The same-tokenizer requirement is implicit, not stated: the student's token ids are passed unchanged to the teacher's `compute_logprobs`, and the only caveat TML writes is about differing *renderers* (chat templates). Every teacher/student pair TML has shown shares a tokenizer family (Qwen3/3.5/3.6, Kimi K2.x with itself, Inkling with Inkling).
- The multi-turn example uses the *same base model* as student and teacher with a fresh rank-8 LoRA (which starts as an identity perturbation), so out of the box the KL starts near zero; it is a wiring demonstration, meant to be run with a different teacher checkpoint or a student `load_checkpoint_path`, not a reported experiment.
- In multi-turn mode the teacher scores each student turn conditioned on the student's own history including real tool outputs, and γ=0 is kept, so the signal is still purely local next-token agreement; no trajectory-level outcome enters unless the user restores a non-zero `reward_fn`.

### Gaps
- No results (KL curves, task success before/after) are published for the Harbor multi-turn recipe or for the multi-teacher recipe.
- The cookbook exposes LoRA only; the blog's full-fine-tuning curves used internal infrastructure (full fine-tuning on Tinker is described as "a beta feature" only in October 2026). The blog-run batch size, rollout length and LR for the full-FT curves are not confirmed by any source.
- Whether `max_tokens=4096` (the 2025 default) was used for the blog's reasoning run is not stated; the 2026 rerun raised it to 16384 and doubled the step count (100 to 200), and no ablation of rollout length is published beyond the qualitative "distillation learns reasonably at shorter context lengths".

## What has TML published since about OPD, and how is it used in the Inkling models?

### Takeaway
TML never published a second OPD research post; instead OPD shows up as a tool in later work, and its role shifts over 2026 from capability transfer to alignment. In order: on-policy context distillation as a suggested project (Nov 2025); multi-turn Harbor OPD and top-K / SDFT recipes in the cookbook (Mar–Apr 2026); OPD as a KL trust-region term added to task reward with a ratcheting self-teacher in the Bridgewater guest post (Jun 2026); Inkling's separate reasoning and agentic RL runs "combined … with multi-teacher distillation" (disclosed only retroactively); Inkling-Small post-trained "in part using on-policy distillation with Inkling as the teacher" (Jul 2026); and, in Inkling 2 Small and Inkling 2 Mini (Nov 2026), "on-policy self-distillation from a teacher model prompted with a conduct spec" used for alignment, while capability merging was replaced by a single joint RL run. Mini's post-pruning "healing" is distillation from a larger internal Inkling model, but TML does not say it is on-policy.

### Inferences
- TML's own production use moved OPD from "cheap substitute for RL" (the 2025 pitch) to two narrower jobs: (i) transferring behaviour between sibling models (Inkling to Inkling-Small in July 2026) and (ii) baking a prompt into weights for alignment (Inkling 2 Small / Mini in November 2026). The second is exactly the "on-policy context distillation" idea TML floated in November 2025: the teacher is the model plus a long system prompt (the conduct spec), the student is the model without it. The citations chosen (OPSD and SDFT, both same-model teacher with privileged context) support this reading; TML does not publish the spec, prompts, data mix, token budget, loss form or coefficient.
- For capability *merging*, TML moved away from distillation: Inkling (July) merged separately RL-trained reasoning and agentic specialists by multi-teacher distillation, whereas Inkling 2 Small (November) did "a single joint RL run" over all domains. TML presents this as a contrast, not as a criticism of distillation, and gives no ablation; but it is the one place where TML replaced a distillation step with something else.
- In Inkling-Small the student ended up *above* its OPD teacher on reasoning and agentic coding. Pure reverse-KL OPD converges to the teacher, so the surplus must come from the other ingredients TML lists (better pre-training mix/recipe, and two further weeks of agentic coding RL after the OPD-initialised checkpoint). The sequencing "OPD first, RL after" matches the blog's view of distillation as "a shortcut" to a known strategy, with RL then doing the search.
- The Bridgewater recipe is a real modification of blog-style OPD, not just a usage: (a) KL is averaged over the sequence and folded into a scalar reward, so it is a sequence-level penalty rather than a per-token dense signal; (b) it is added to a task reward with weight β, which is the "combining distillation-based per-token rewards with sequence-level environment rewards" the blog left as future work (in trajectory-level form); (c) the teacher is not a stronger model but the student's own best checkpoint, promoted on validation accuracy — a ratcheting trust region. The section title says "strong teachers" but the teachers are the base model and earlier checkpoints of the same fine-tune. β and the exact loss (CISPO with asymmetric clipping) hyperparameters are not given.
- Does TML's production OPD use logits or sampled tokens? Not stated anywhere for Inkling. Evidence on TML's general direction: the 2025 blog used sampled tokens only and flagged top-k logit distillation as an efficiency upgrade; in April 2026 the cookbook added top-K (K=20) soft-target losses and a top-K reverse-KL custom loss; in September–October 2026 Tinker shipped "top-k logprobs for distillation and scoring". Internally TML has full logits available, so nothing constrains it to the sampled-token estimator, but which was used for Inkling is unknown.
- Is OPD applied to multi-turn/tool-use agent trajectories? In the cookbook, yes since 2026-03-10 (Harbor/Terminal-Bench sandboxes, student-token masking, zero reward), with no published results. For Inkling, Inkling-Small's OPD stage preceded "agentic coding RL" and the alignment OPD in Inkling 2 Small targets misbehaviour that arose on long agentic tasks (fabricating results, concealing actions), which makes agentic rollouts a natural training distribution; but TML does not say what prompts or environments the OPD phases ran on.
- "Healing after pruning" in Inkling 2 Mini is described as training "on 1T tokens distilled from a larger internal Inkling model", placed at mid-training and measured in tokens like the pre/mid-training phases. That phrasing fits teacher-generated data or teacher-forced logit distillation on a corpus at least as well as student-rollout OPD; it should not be reported as on-policy distillation.

### Gaps
- No TML post, model card or doc gives any quantitative detail of OPD inside Inkling: no token/step budget, no teacher identity beyond "Inkling" / "a teacher model prompted with a conduct spec", no loss, no coefficient, no before/after metric isolating the OPD phase, no statement on logits vs sampled tokens, and no statement on whether rollouts were multi-turn.
- Whether Inkling's "multi-teacher distillation" was on-policy is not stated (the cookbook's April 2026 "MOPD" docstring and off-policy top-K "multi-teacher knowledge merging" module show TML had both variants in code at the time, which is suggestive of either).
- The conduct spec is not published in the Inkling 2 Small post or model card; "A Safe Path to Open Weights" (2026-07-31) mentions testing against "our model spec" but contains no mention of distillation.
- I found no talks, podcasts or X threads in which TML researchers (Kevin Lu, John Schulman, Lilian Weng, Alex Gartrell or others) add technical detail on OPD or its Inkling usage; individual X timelines are not indexed by the search tool and could not be fetched. This is an access gap, not evidence that no such statements exist.
- Chinese-language searches surfaced only third-party restatements of the official posts (a 36Kr piece on the 2025 post and coverage of Inkling-Small repeating the "Inkling as the teacher" sentence appeared in results; I did not open them); no additional TML-sourced detail was found.
- A search-result summary claimed John Schulman co-authored "On-policy Distillation with Verifiable Reward" (arXiv 2608.24696); the arXiv page lists Wenze Lin et al. (Tsinghua LeapLab), so this is an external paper and the attribution was a search-summary error.

## What limitations and open issues does TML itself acknowledge?

### Takeaway
TML's stated caveats are mostly about preconditions and accounting rather than failure modes: OPD needs log-prob access to the teacher and a student whose support already covers the teacher's behaviour (hence SFT/mid-training first and bigger batches when it does not); it cannot teach knowledge and converges to, not beyond, the teacher; its headline efficiency numbers rest on an extrapolated SFT baseline, a borrowed RL number and a FLOP metric; and logit/top-k distillation and mixing KL with environment reward were left as future work. TML has not published any analysis of teacher unreliability on student-visited states, and it never states the same-tokenizer requirement explicitly.

### Cited Findings

Preconditions
- Teacher access: "Any instruction-tuned open-weight model can be used as a reward model in this sense; we merely need access to the `compute_logprobs` function." — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Tinker itself exposes sampled-token and top-K log-probs, not full logits: "The Tinker API does not expose full-vocabulary logits." — [cookbook SDFT README](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/tree/main/tinker_cookbook/recipes/sdft)
- Student support / initialisation: mid-training "increases the probability that the student will generate tokens within the teacher's distribution, though it's usually far from sufficient for replicating the teacher's performance"; "Reverse-KL methods can then perform mode seeking within the initialization's support. Often, as we'll see in our personalization example, the probability of generating the relevant tokens starts at zero as the student lacks any relevant domain knowledge." — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Batch size depends on that support: when the teacher policy is not within the student's support, "as in 'Distillation for reasoning', we require a significantly larger batch size." — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Renderer mismatch: "if your teacher has a different renderer than the student, you may want to modify the full_sequence_inputs_D to match the teacher's renderer." — [train_on_policy.py](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/blob/main/tinker_cookbook/distillation/train_on_policy.py)

What OPD does not do
- No new knowledge from on-policy shaping: "RL only shapes behavior—it cannot teach new knowledge well, and thus can't be sufficient for continual learning"; in the personalization experiment knowledge comes from the SFT mid-train and OPD only restores behaviour. — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Ceiling at the teacher: "since the teacher stays fixed, the student converges on the teacher's desirable behavior"; reverse KL "learns one specific behavior (the teacher's)". — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Pre-training is not distillable this way: "Pre-training requires a vast amount of information and is very difficult to distill, in part because the parameter space is somewhat unique to each network." — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- In Inkling-Small, the distilled model still trails its teacher where capacity matters: "Inkling maintains an advantage on knowledge coverage and factuality." — [TML News: Introducing Inkling-Small](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-small/)

Caveats on the evidence
- SFT baseline is extrapolated: "This extrapolation requires the scaling law to hold up without stalling, which isn't trivial… We can use this extrapolation as an optimistic estimate for the cost-performance ratio of off-policy distillation." — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- RL baseline is Qwen3's, not a matched run: "It is hard to compare this directly to the cost of distillation." — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Cost metric: "Comparing compute costs across methods is nontrivial, as the ratio of training vs sampling vs log-prob computation cost varies significantly depending on implementation"; FLOPs "overestimates the practical cost of computing log-probs." — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Teacher mismatch in the headline run: the teacher actually used was Qwen3-8B while FLOPs were computed for Qwen3-32B (footnote). — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Single-prompt training "is naturally less compute-efficient." — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Scope disclaimer: "Our work explored simple and straightforward instantiations of on-policy distillation to clearly showcase its advantages. We hope to continue research on new applications of distillation, new methods for improving teacher supervision, and ways to improve data efficiency and continual learning." — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)

Design choices flagged as open
- Discount factor > 0 is "more mathematically correct" but showed no benefit. — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Logit distillation not explored: "we do not consider logit (top-k) distillation in any of our experiments, which could be used to further improve compute efficiency." — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Hybrid reward not explored: "There could be advantages to combining distillation-based per-token rewards with sequence-level environment rewards; this is an interesting area for potential future research." — [TML: On-Policy Distillation](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- Multi-teacher OPD shipped in code but "we do not showcase this in our blog post"; the multi-turn recipe is presented as an extension because the base recipes are "single-turn". — [cookbook distillation README](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/tree/main/tinker_cookbook/recipes/distillation)

Failure modes and fixes TML has documented in adjacent recipes
- Distilling toward a stale or weaker teacher: the Bridgewater recipe only promotes a checkpoint to teacher "if validation accuracy has reached a new high, so we never distill toward a weaker model"; a frozen base-model teacher was 3.1 points worse. — [TML News: Learning to Replicate Expert Judgment in Financial Tasks](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/learning-to-replicate-expert-judgment-in-financial-tasks/)
- SDFT learning-rate sensitivity and model compatibility: tool-use retention falls to 52% at LR 1e-3 (vs 94–97% at 1e-4/5e-4); "it is likely to work poorly out of the box with Qwen3.5/3.6 long-thinking models unless the SFT demonstrations are regenerated for those models"; the recipe "does not cover learning from environment feedback." — [cookbook SDFT README](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/tree/main/tinker_cookbook/recipes/sdft)
- Bugs fixed in the OPD path: missing temperature parameter (2025-11-24, #103); undefined `get_reference_answer` in `PromptOnlyEnv` (2025-11-03, #68); checkpoint loading not restoring optimiser state/step count (2026-03-13, #446); the SDFT reverse-KL path was reworked on 2026-04-21 (#677). — [tinker-cookbook commit history for the distillation directories](https://github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook/commits/main/tinker_cookbook/distillation)

Residual problems after alignment OPD (TML does not attribute these to OPD)
- Inkling 2 Small "sometimes overreacts with disclaimers in normal emotional support queries"; FORTRESS over-refusal (ORS) 20.4 vs 3.2–3.6 for GLM 5.3 models; limitations list "overreaction to mental health and other safety related queries." — [TML News: Introducing Inkling 2 Small](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-small/)

### Inferences
- Of the four limitations the brief asked about: (1) "teacher must expose log-probs" is acknowledged directly; (2) "same tokenizer" is only implicit in the code (token ids are passed straight to the teacher; the sole written caveat concerns renderers), and every TML pairing stays within one tokenizer family; (3) "teacher unreliable on student states" is not discussed by TML at all — the post's framing is the opposite ("unhackable… low KL always corresponds to a high probability of desirable behavior from the teacher model's point of view"), with the SimpleBench illustration as the only qualitative look at per-token teacher scores; (4) "multi-turn tool environments" are acknowledged as outside the original single-turn recipes and addressed by the March 2026 Harbor recipe, without results.
- The "unhackable" claim is conditional on the teacher's judgement being right at student-visited states; TML's own later practice hedges it — the Bridgewater recipe keeps a task reward and gates teacher promotion on validation accuracy, and Inkling 2 Small pairs alignment OPD with an RL alignment reward, trace filtering and targeted environments rather than relying on the teacher's likelihood alone.
- Because the estimator scores only sampled tokens with γ=0, two things follow that TML notes only obliquely: a wrong final answer that is "entirely predictable conditional on the whole preceding sequence" receives no penalty (credit goes to earlier "forking tokens"), and the signal says nothing about tokens the student never samples — which is why initial support and batch size matter so much.
- Over-refusal in Inkling 2 Small is a plausible side effect of distilling toward a conduct-spec-prompted teacher (a spec-prompted model tends to be more cautious everywhere), but this is my conjecture; the same post lists three other alignment interventions that could equally be responsible.

### Gaps
- No TML analysis of: teacher calibration or error on out-of-distribution student prefixes; behaviour when teacher and student differ greatly in capacity (the only large-gap pairs are the qualitative Qwen3-4B/235B illustration and Inkling to Inkling-Small, neither with OPD-specific metrics); length or repetition pathologies under reverse KL; or interaction with reasoning-effort conditioning in Inkling.
- No statement on cross-tokenizer or closed-API teachers (a [Hugging Face staff launch post for Inkling](https://huggingface.co/blog/thinkingmachines-inkling) suggests the GOLD algorithm in TRL for cross-tokenizer distillation with Inkling as teacher, but that is not a TML disclosure).
- No published negative results for OPD from TML; the only regressions shown are for SFT baselines and for SDFT at a high learning rate.
