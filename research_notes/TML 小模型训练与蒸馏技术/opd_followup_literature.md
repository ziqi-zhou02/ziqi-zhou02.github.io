## Q1. What are the 2026 OPD papers, and what does each one modify?

### Takeaway
The prior survey's "~10 unread siblings" turned out to be the visible tip of a much larger body: two curated lists and three surveys now index well over 100 OPD papers from 2026. For multi-turn agents the variants cluster into six fixes: (1) control rollout depth or where the student starts (TCOD, TurnOPD, STRIDE, ReOPD, Prune-OPD, ADWIN, POPD/TOPD); (2) let the teacher act on some turns (Guided-OPD, SAGE-OPD, FutureBridge, OLIVE, Step-level OPD); (3) validate teacher guidance by outcome before trusting it (OG-OPD, FutureBridge, RA-OPD, OPDVR, GraphOPD); (4) clean up what the teacher is conditioned on (State-OPD, MemOPD); (5) mix or anneal OPD with RL reward (SDAR, RetireOPD, ATOD); (6) multi-teacher specialist merging (MOPD and descendants). Only a handful test on SWE-bench / Terminal-Bench class tasks (SWE-OPD, OPRD, CAMP-II, COPD; see Q2).

**C. Long-horizon coding / terminal agent OPD (detail in Q2)**
- **SWE-OPD** — "SWE-OPD: On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Coding", Yuhang He, Yanyu Chen, Chuanzhe Guo, Jie Zhu et al. (HKUST-Guangzhou, CUHK, HIT, Baidu ERNIE team), arXiv 2611.21020, 16 Nov 2026. Top-K reverse KL (K = 128, normalised over the student's top-K) versus sampled-token policy-gradient loss; an offline variant caches the teacher's top-1024 tokens once; a cross-tokenizer offline variant re-encodes tokens to the teacher's tokenizer. **[full]** — [arXiv 2611.21020](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.21020)
- **OPRD** — "On-Policy Reminder Distillation for Long-Horizon Coding Agents", Yuechen Luo, … Ken Deng (Tsinghua, Shanghai AI Lab, NUS), arXiv 2610.21496, 20 Oct 2026. Self-teacher of the same architecture sees "Multi-Scope Reminders" (global, from final-test diagnostics; turn-local, tied to one observation) as privileged information and is periodically reset to the student's weights; failed rollouts get reverse KL (top-100 support), verified-successful rollouts get self-imitation. **[full]** — [arXiv 2610.21496](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21496)
- **CAMP-II** — "Coding Agent Memory Post-Training II", Lirui Luo, Kelong Mao, … Cong Fang (PKU, JD.com and others), arXiv 2611.14009, 12 Nov 2026. Frozen Qwen3.8-27B teacher distils file-based memory behaviour (write a continuation file before context replacement, read it back) into Qwen3.5-4B / 9B with fully asynchronous OPD and no task reward. **[full]** — [arXiv 2611.14009](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14009)
- **COPD** — "Communicative On-Policy Distillation: Distilling Black-Box Teacher Capabilities through Communication for Long-Horizon Tasks", same group, arXiv 2611.23673, 18 Nov 2026. The student decides when to ask a text-only teacher; successful exchanges become privileged references for on-policy self-distillation plus task-reward RL. **[abstract; HTML returned 404]** — [arXiv 2611.23673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23673)

**D. Rollout-length and compute-efficiency variants (single-turn reasoning)**
- **Prune-OPD** (Zhicheng Yang, Zhijiang Guo et al.; HKUST-GZ, HKUST, MBZUAI, UC Merced, SYSU), arXiv 2605.07804, v1 8 May 2026. Monitors top-16 overlap between student and teacher; a drift event fires below 0.7 overlap; cumulative drift down-weights later tokens and a controller shrinks or grows the rollout budget (1,024–12,288 tokens). Training-time reductions of 37.6–68.0% on four low-compatibility pairs with accuracy preserved; only 2.9% on a high-compatibility pair. **[full]** — [arXiv 2605.07804](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07804)
- **ADWIN** (Kun Liang et al.), arXiv 2605.28396, 27 May 2026. Train on short teacher-anchored prefixes, use delayed full-rollout probes to adapt the horizon; "up to 4.1× lower end-to-end training cost". **[abstract]** — [arXiv 2605.28396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28396)
- **POPD / TOPD** — "Are Full Rollouts Necessary for On-Policy Distillation?" (Yaocheng Zhang, … Dongbin Zhao), arXiv 2605.31490, 29 May 2026. Progressive OPD improves training efficiency by up to 3×; Truncated OPD matches OPD using 10% of the rollout horizon (math reasoning). **[abstract]** — [arXiv 2605.31490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31490)
- **Trajectory-Refined Distillation (TRD)** (Li Jiang, Haoran Xu, Yichuan Ding, Amy Zhang), arXiv 2606.08432, v1 7 Jun 2026. Names "prefix failure"; revises the student's rollouts under teacher guidance before distilling. **[abstract]** — [arXiv 2606.08432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08432)
- **LGR** — "Your Teacher Can't Help You Here" (Yanjiang Liu, … Yaojie Lu; UCAS, ISCAS, Xiaohongshu), arXiv 2605.30833, 29 May 2026. Adds a Lookahead Group Reward: score the student's top-8 candidate tokens by the teacher's max-probability at the next position, applied only at high-entropy positions. **[full; previously abstract-only]** — [arXiv 2605.30833](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30833)

**E. Divergence choice, logit support and loss form**
- Top-K reverse KL versus sampled-token loss on long coding trajectories: SWE-OPD finds neither dominates (Q2); increasing K from 128 to 1,024 gives no consistent benefit. — [arXiv 2611.21020](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.21020)
- Reverse KL beats forward KL for a coding agent: average 47.9 vs 46.7 with outcome routing, 46.0 vs 45.2 without (OPRD, Qwen3.5-4B). Top-100 support at temperature 1.0 (47.9) beats full vocabulary at T = 0.6 (46.5). — [arXiv 2610.21496](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21496)
- Mixed divergences by who generated the turn: reverse KL on student turns + forward KL on teacher turns scores 37.94 average Score vs 36.58 for reverse KL on both and 34.56 for forward KL on both (Guided-OPD, Qwen3-1.7B). — [arXiv 2606.15912](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15912)
- **TS-OPD** (Xiaoyu Chen et al.), arXiv 2610.05974, 5 Oct 2026: route problems the student is still acquiring to gated forward KL, problems it mostly solves to gated reverse KL, plus an "entropy brake". **[abstract]** — [arXiv 2610.05974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05974)
- **OC-OPD** (Ruoran Xu, Yilun Zhao, Ziyu Chen, Arman Cohan), arXiv 2611.01965, 1 Nov 2026: treats reverse KL (sharpens ranking among the student's likely tokens) and forward KL (moves mass to teacher-supported candidates) as complementary and combines their gradients. **[abstract]** — [arXiv 2611.01965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01965)
- Entropy-Aware OPD (Jin et al., 2026) switches between reverse and forward KL by entropy and is reported by the survey at +5.05 Pass@8 over reverse KL on Qwen3-4B-Base; in SAGE-OPD's ALFWorld table it is the best method in one of four settings (79.10 unseen vs 73.88 for SAGE-OPD). — [survey 2604.00626](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00626v1); [SAGE-OPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19659)
- Industrial disagreement on full-vocabulary versus sampled-token: DeepSeek-V4 uses full-vocabulary logits; Nemotron 3 Ultra reports full-distribution or top-k distillation "did not improve MOPD" and underperformed the sampled-token objective on some agentic benchmarks (Q4). — [DeepSeek-V4 report](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19348); [Nemotron 3 Ultra](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15007)
- Listings not opened: "Revisiting On-Policy Distillation" (2603.25562, truncated reverse KL with teacher top-K support), "Divergence controls entropy in distillation" (2610.03529), "A Token-Level Analysis of Sampled-Token Reverse-KL OPD" (2608.25643), "Escaping the KL Agreement Trap in OPD" (2606.09471). **[listing]** — [awesome-on-policy-distillation](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-on-policy-distillation)

**F. Black-box / text-only teachers (no log-probs)**

### Inferences
- The field's centre of mass moved in 2026 from "which divergence" to "which student states and which teacher signals to trust". Almost every agent-OPD paper is a different estimator of teacher reliability at a student state (depth, overlap, confidence, entropy, paired outcome).
- Most agent-OPD papers still share one evaluation template: Qwen3 0.6B–4B students, a Qwen3-8B/30B/32B teacher, and ALFWorld / WebShop / ScienceWorld. Rankings among them are not stable across papers (see Q3), so a researcher targeting a 20–30B student should treat them as a menu of ideas, not as validated recipes.
- Two late-2026 papers (SWE-OPD, OPRD) and one industrial report (KAT-Coder-V2.5) independently converge on the same recipe for long coding trajectories: off-policy cold start, reverse KL on a top-k support of about 100–128 tokens, and some form of drift- or outcome-aware masking.

### Gaps
- Not opened at all: the ~35 listing-only papers named above. Their one-line descriptions come from a community-maintained list and are unverified.
- The survey's Sections 6–9 (industrial deployments, open problems, guidelines) and its reference list were beyond the readable window in both v1 and v4.
- I found no 2026 OPD paper specifically about distilling the teacher's thinking/reasoning tokens for agents beyond what State-OPD (strip the student's prior reasoning from the teacher's context) and Nemotron/Muse (reasoning-budget control by RL) report. OPSDC (reasoning compression, 41–59% shorter reasoning per the survey) is single-turn and was not read. — [survey 2604.00626](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00626v1)

## Q2. Which papers test on genuinely long-horizon agentic benchmarks, and what are the numbers versus SFT, RL and the teacher?

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026 I found four academic OPD papers on SWE-bench / Terminal-Bench class tasks (SWE-OPD, OPRD, CAMP-II, COPD), one on τ²-bench (STRIDE), and four industrial reports with before/after-OPD agentic numbers (MiMo-V2-Flash, MOPD on Qwen3-30B-A3B, Nemotron 3 Ultra, plus Muse Glimmer without an ablation). ## Q3. What failure modes are documented, and which fixes have evidence?

### Takeaway
Seven failure modes now have numbers behind them: (1) teacher supervision decays on long student-written prefixes; (2) the teacher's token-level preference is frequently wrong about outcomes; (3) reverse-KL OPD can collapse into long repetitive output while the loss looks healthy; (4) vanilla OPD can fail outright for very small students in multi-turn environments; (5) OPD mostly reweights what the student can already do; (6) a stronger or larger teacher often yields a worse student; (7) scoring long contexts is expensive and easy to get subtly wrong. Fixes with repeated evidence: SFT cold start, depth/prefix curricula, outcome- or reward-gating of the teacher signal, masking truncated or drifted spans, and choosing the teacher by compatibility, not strength. No fix has been validated at 20–30B student scale on long-horizon tasks.

### Cited Findings

**1. Teacher unreliable on off-distribution student states**
- Teacher advantage on continuations drops from +0.37 at a 1K-token student prefix to +0.02 at a 16K prefix; in a response-length sweep, 3K and 7K limits train best and 10K and 15K plateau or decline with late-stage overlap collapse. — [Rethinking OPD, arXiv 2604.13016](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13016)
- "Supervision Fidelity Decay": teacher completion accuracy falls monotonically with student prefix length; a sigmoid fits the decay with R² > 0.997 and a supervision boundary near 7.0–7.4k tokens for a 7B/32B pair; OPD training itself moves the boundary earlier (7.43k → 7.04k). LGR's advantage over OPD appears only at long budgets: on a 1.5B student, AIME-25 mean@8 is 32.92 vs 33.33 (LGR vs OPD) at 3k tokens but 36.75 vs 32.08 at 39k. — [arXiv 2605.30833](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30833)
- In multi-turn agents, teacher perplexity on student-generated tokens rises with turn index and barely improves between training steps 100 and 200 under vanilla OPD. — [Guided-OPD, arXiv 2606.15912](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15912)
- Per-turn KL under vanilla OPD starts around 1,000 versus a converged value near 60 and grows with turn index. — [TCOD, arXiv 2604.24005](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24005)
- For Qwen3 pairs, the effective reliable OPD window can shrink to a few hundred tokens against a 12,288-token budget, with drifted suffix tokens dominating gradients (authors' analysis, Appendix A.11). — [Prune-OPD, arXiv 2605.07804](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07804)
- Industrial confirmation: Muse Glimmer switches from teacher logits to cross-entropy in mid-training Phase 2 because "the distributional shift introduced by long-context and agentic data makes teacher logits less reliable at this stage"; KAT-Coder-V2.5 names loss oscillation, entropy collapse and gradient-norm spikes on long contexts (no numbers). — [arXiv 2610.24628](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24628); [arXiv 2607.05471](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05471)
- Fixes with evidence: depth curriculum (TCOD: Qwen2.5-3B ALFWorld unseen 60.45 → 79.19; about 32% less training time); teacher-turn mixing with decay to zero (Guided-OPD: +21.1% Score and +25.5% success rate relative to vanilla OPD on average); overlap-based drift down-weighting and truncation (Prune-OPD: 37.6–68.0% less time, accuracy preserved; also adopted in KAT-Coder-V2.5); prefix replay with early-step bias (ReOPD: math average 57.2 vs 55.1 for OPD, ≥ 4× faster per rollout, zero tool calls). — [TCOD](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24005); [Guided-OPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15912); [Prune-OPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07804); [ReOPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04763)

**2. Teacher's dense signal disagrees with task outcome**
- Under standard sampled-token OPD on math, the trajectory-level distillation return is negative for 94.25% of trajectories (Qwen3 setting); early in training nearly all correct trajectories are penalised and nearly all incorrect ones are reinforced by the reward-agreement criterion. Masking reward-misaligned trajectories (RA-OPD) drops 48.63% of trajectories and lifts avg@k from 40.68 to 45.88 (Qwen3-4B-Base) and 44.34 to 49.43 (Qwen3-8B-Base) at 4.48 h vs 4.38 h training time. — [arXiv 2608.27960](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27960)
- Teacher advantages point the wrong way on verifiable answer tokens 30.6% of the time with a Qwen3-4B teacher, 34.7% with 30B-A3B and 50.6% with 235B-A22B; the largest teacher gave negative advantages to 97.8% of correct answer tokens. — [arXiv 2608.31046](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.31046)
- In multi-turn agents, among large teacher–student-gap decisions the teacher's action hurt in 10.8% of cases and rescued in 8.8% (ScienceWorld + WebShop average); 7.6% of small-gap decisions were outcome-critical. — [OG-OPD, arXiv 2609.35319](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35319)
- Executing the teacher's action at the highest-disagreement turn of 1,000 failed trajectories improved the outcome in 30.9% of cases (random turn: 5.7%); on graded-score tasks it degraded the outcome in 19.0%. — [FutureBridge-OPD, arXiv 2608.01953](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01953)
- Sequence-mean teacher reward separates correct from incorrect rollouts with AUROC of only 0.73–0.75; a rubric reward reaches AUC 0.90 where teacher log-likelihood gets 0.35. — [arXiv 2604.13016](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13016); [ROPD, arXiv 2605.07396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07396)
- Fixes with evidence: outcome-validated up-weighting (OG-OPD beats vanilla OPD by 3.6–17.7 success-rate points across five settings at 1.42–1.48× per-step cost); future-validated bridges (FutureBridge +16.6 average SR over OPD for a 1.7B student, 3 seeds); reward gating (RA-OPD above; OPDVR's ReLU gate, abstract only). — [OG-OPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35319); [FutureBridge](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01953); [OPDVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24696)

**3. Collapse, repetition and "student exploits the teacher"**
- A Qwen3-4B → Qwen3-1.7B-Base run converges normally by loss and advantage curves, yet 99.4% of responses hit the generation limit, 38% show severe repetition, and accuracy ends at 5.4% versus 19.6% for the teacher. Before training, the teacher already assigns its highest advantage to long repetitive student responses it rarely generates itself. The same failure occurs with 8B and 30B-A3B teachers. — [Gains and Collapse, arXiv 2610.03185](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03185)
- On long coding trajectories, the GLM-4.5-Air ← GLM-5.2 top-K reverse-KL run collapsed into repetition as entropy fell while training loss kept decreasing; the authors call this "support escape" (probability moves to unscored tail tokens). The sampled-token loss did not collapse on the same pair. — [SWE-OPD, arXiv 2611.21020](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.21020)
- Two length-exploitation modes: "endless exploration" dilutes negative advantage with filler tokens; "abrupt degeneration" truncates early to keep a favourable prefix. — [arXiv 2607.13399](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13399)
- Over-weighting a teacher-derived bonus causes reward hacking through repetitive text (LGR with γ = 10: AIME-24 37.08 vs 46.67 at γ = 1). — [arXiv 2605.30833](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30833)
- Theory: with a mixture of teachers, the reverse-KL target is a geometric mean and can push the correct-set probability below any η > 0 if one low-weight teacher is misleading, while forward KL keeps at least w_k·p_k of the mass; with long horizons the reverse-KL target can come to favour an incorrect first token (Proposition 6.5). No experiments. — [arXiv 2609.38666](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38666)
- Fixes with evidence: SFT warm-up avoids the collapse entirely (16.38% average accuracy, 6.22 points above the base-initialised run); masking truncated responses adds 3.08 / 0.51 / 2.58 points for 4B / 8B / 30B-A3B teachers; hard clipping or log-scaling of the log-ratio adds +0.5 to +2.3 average points; MOPD clips the advantage at ±5 and SDAR/OpenClaw-RL clip the log-probability difference. — [arXiv 2610.03185](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03185); [arXiv 2607.13399](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13399); [MOPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30406v1); [OpenClaw-RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10165)

**4. Vanilla OPD fails outright for small students in multi-turn environments**
- Qwen3-1.7B with a Qwen3-30B-A3B teacher under vanilla OPD: 0.17% average validation success (WebShop 0.14, ALFWorld 0.32, ScienceWorld 0.05) versus 18.84% with TCOD; the 4B student does not collapse (27.64% vs teacher 30.28%). — [TCOD, arXiv 2604.24005](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24005)
- Standalone on-policy self-distillation without RL collapses in agents: 28.1 / 32.8 / 14.1 on ALFWorld for Qwen2.5-3B / 7B / Qwen3-1.7B versus 75.0 / 81.2 / 46.1 for GRPO; naive GRPO+OPSD on Qwen3-1.7B scores 32.0, below GRPO's 46.1. — [SDAR, arXiv 2605.15155](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15155)
- Memory-action OPD without an SFT cold start scores 0.0% because the student never samples a successful answer, so OPD has no behaviour to supervise. — [CAMP-II, arXiv 2611.14009](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14009)
- SFT on teacher traces is far weaker than any OPD variant in these environments (ALFWorld seen, 3B student: SFT 32.14 vs OPD 65.72 vs TCOD 81.43; SAGE-OPD's 0.6B student: SFT 2.86 vs OPD 55.00). — [TCOD](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24005); [SAGE-OPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19659)

**5. OPD reweights existing behaviour more than it adds capability**
- OPD raises pass@1 but does not expand the solvable set: the initial student solves 73 AIME24–26 problems at a large sampling budget, the OPD students 65 and 63, and no problem is solved only by an OPD student. — [arXiv 2610.03185](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03185)
- Pass@k curves of OPD and base students converge at high k; at fixed budget, more distinct prompts with one rollout each beat more rollouts per prompt. — [arXiv 2607.13399](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13399)
- Training only on the lowest-20% log-probability tokens matches full-token OPD, and a teacher-free fixed negative advantage on those tokens also improves AIME24; the teacher-free method OPSA reports 48.85 AIME24 Avg@32 on Qwen3-1.7B versus 32.08 for OPD (students ≤ 9B, math only). — [arXiv 2608.31046](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.31046)
- A single teacher token flip at a fork raises success only from 9.91% to 11.31%; teacher segments of 16 / 128 tokens / to-the-end add 3.25 / 16.51 / 79.68 points; flipping about 90% of forks takes a 28.4% student to 74.5% against the teacher's 76.9%. — [arXiv 2610.19145](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19145)
- Nemotron 3 Ultra: MOPD works best when the teacher's advantage is expressed as token-level preferences over trajectories the student can already sample (tool use, abstention, multi-step execution) and recovers little on HLE (16.9% of the gap). — [arXiv 2606.15007](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15007)

**6. Capacity gap and teacher mismatch** — see Q5 for the size-ratio evidence.

**7. Cost and correctness of teacher scoring on long contexts**
- SWE-OPD cost split (offline, GPU-hours, Qwen / GLM): rollout 176 / 368, teacher scoring 48 / 160, training 192 / 240. Caching the teacher's top-1024 once cuts total cost 2.46× / 3.65×. — [arXiv 2611.21020](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.21020)
- Rollout time, not teacher scoring, dominates multi-turn OPD: full OPD takes 273.8 s per step on τ²-bench retail; STRIDE reaches 3.0–3.7× speed-up at equal accuracy; TurnOPD 1.26–2.29×; ReOPD removes environment calls. — [STRIDE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14636); [TurnOPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05804v2); [ReOPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04763)
- Context-alignment bugs silently corrupt the signal: with flattened persistent history in a compact-memory agent, the teacher's top-1 prediction changes at 651 action positions and 13.29% of tokens are falsely PPO-clipped before any update; using trajectory or response masks instead of action masks would add 66,830–74,357 spurious positions against 12,083 true action tokens. — [MemOPD, arXiv 2608.07068](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07068)
- Conditioning the teacher on the student's earlier reasoning distorts supervision; removing it lifts a Qwen3-4B student's macro-average from 26.07 to 31.74 (teacher 33.29). — [State-OPD, arXiv 2610.13238](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13238)
- Systems answers from industry: asynchronous teacher prefill services so scoring overlaps with sampling (MOPD reports nearly no added wall-clock); DeepSeek-V4 caches only last-layer teacher hidden states and rebuilds full logits per teacher head; GLM-5 uses a token-in-token-out gateway to avoid re-tokenisation mismatch. — [MOPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30406v1); [DeepSeek-V4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19348); [GLM-5](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15763)

**Instability of method rankings across papers**
- TCOD-F2B is below vanilla OPD in TurnOPD's ALFWorld table (80.06 vs 83.00 for 1.7B; 86.50 vs 91.81 for 4B) and in STRIDE's τ²-bench table (0.437 vs 0.477). Guided-OPD is below TCOD-B2F in OG-OPD's 4B ALFWorld column (57.1 vs 62.6) and has standard deviations up to ±10.6 in FutureBridge's three-seed table. SOD and TCOD are within about 2 points of vanilla OPD in ATOD's table. — [TurnOPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05804v2); [STRIDE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14636); [OG-OPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35319); [FutureBridge](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01953); [ATOD](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27814)
- The same nominal baseline varies widely by setup: vanilla OPD on ALFWorld for a Qwen3-1.7B student is 83.00 with an RL-trained 8B teacher (TurnOPD), 72.66 with a GRPO 4B teacher (ATOD), and about 20–24 with a zero-shot 30B/32B teacher (Guided-OPD, FutureBridge, OG-OPD). — [TurnOPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05804v2); [ATOD](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27814); [OG-OPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35319)

### Inferences
- The single most reproducible fix is not a loss change: start OPD from a student that already samples the target behaviour (SFT cold start), and use a teacher that is itself competent in the environment. The largest swing in the ALFWorld literature (about 20% → 83% for the same student and algorithm) comes from replacing a zero-shot large teacher with a smaller RL-trained one.
- "Mode collapse from reverse KL" in 2026 evidence is less about lost diversity and more about degenerate length/repetition and entropy collapse. I found no paper that measures output-diversity loss of OPD students on agentic tasks directly.
- Findings 2 and 5 together suggest that on tasks with a verifier, pure OPD is leaving signal unused; every method that brings outcome information in (RA-OPD, OPDVR, OG-OPD, OPRD's outcome routing, ATOD) reports gains.
- The "OPD only reweights" finding is from ≤ 9B students on competition math. Whether it holds for agentic skills is open; the Nemotron remark about HLE versus tool use points the same way.

### Gaps
- None of the analysis papers (Gains and Collapse, Does OPD Really Distill, both Demystifying papers, Shape of Uncertainty) includes a multi-turn tool-using task; all are single-turn math or instruction following.
- No paper measures a student learning to exploit a teacher's blind spots in a tool-using environment (for example, actions the teacher rates highly but that fail in the environment) beyond the 10.8% "teacher harm" statistic.
- No evidence at 128K+ contexts about how supervision decay behaves; SWE-OPD trains at 128K but gives no decay analysis.

## Q4. How is OPD used in industrial pipelines disclosed in 2026?

### Takeaway
By late 2026, OPD is a standard post-training stage in at least a dozen disclosed model recipes, and its dominant industrial use is consolidation: train same-lineage domain specialists with SFT + RL, then distil them into one student on the student's own rollouts (MiMo-V2-Flash, DeepSeek-V4, Nemotron 3 Ultra, KAT-Coder-V2/V2.5). Strong-to-weak compression into a small model (Qwen3 small models, Muse Glimmer 30B) and self-distillation from a privileged context (Cursor Composer 2.5) are the other two patterns. The reports disagree on objective (full-vocabulary KL versus sampled-token estimate) and on stage order (OPD before RL, after RL, or instead of mixed RL).

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Three stage orders coexist in production: OPD before RL as a bridge (Muse Glimmer: SFT → OPD → RL); OPD after RL as consolidation (DeepSeek-V4, V4.1-Flash, Nemotron 3 Ultra, GLM-5, MiMo); OPD inside RL as a localised auxiliary loss (Composer 2.5, KAT-Coder-V2). The first is the pattern that matches "compress a big model into a 30B student"; the second assumes same-lineage specialists.
- For a team with only API access to a frontier teacher, none of the industrial recipes applies directly: all of them use white-box teachers they trained themselves. The closest transferable ideas are Composer's hint-conditioned self-teacher and the black-box methods in Q1-F.
- Qwen3-Coder-Next should not be cited as evidence for on-policy distillation specifically: the report says "expert distillation" without stating that the student's rollouts are used.
- The sampled-token versus full-vocabulary disagreement (DeepSeek versus NVIDIA) is unresolved by public ablation; SWE-OPD's result that reverse KL collapsed on one pair while sampled-token did not, and won on another metric elsewhere, suggests the answer is pair-dependent.

### Gaps
- No industrial report read here gives an ablation of OPD on a long-horizon benchmark for a small standalone student except the before/after tables of MiMo and Nemotron (large MoE students).
- Muse Glimmer's OPD objective details (KL direction, support size), any ablation numbers for SFT → OPD → RL versus SFT → RL, and the teacher's benchmark scores were not found in the portions read (first ~100k characters plus the appendix chunk).
- DeepSeek-V4's last ~50k characters (appendices) were not read; the specialist count is given only as "more than ten".
- Cursor's blog gives no scale numbers for the KL term, no hint-generation procedure and no ablation.

## Q5. Is there theory or evidence on combining OPD with RL, and on how teacher–student size ratio affects outcomes?

### Takeaway
On OPD + RL there is consistent small-scale evidence for three rules: put sparse-reward RL on the model that can explore (the teacher) and transfer densely; keep the distillation term small and gated when it is an auxiliary to RL (coefficients of 0.01–0.02 recur); and reduce or remove the teacher late in training (annealing or retirement beat a constant mix). On size ratio there is no scaling law; the evidence says teacher size and teacher score are poor predictors of student gain, RL-trained same-lineage teachers transfer best, and several papers see a bigger teacher produce a worse student. All of this is from students ≤ 9B (mostly ≤ 4B) except the MOPD and industrial results.

### Cited Findings

**OPD + RL: ordering**
- Sparse-to-dense principle (LinkedIn/Google, arXiv 2605.12483, v5 12 Oct 2026): GRPO on the teacher → forward-KL warm-up on teacher rollouts → reverse-KL OPD on student rollouts → optional GRPO on the student. Qwen3-1.7B on MATH: 79.3% via an RL-improved Qwen3-8B teacher versus 75.9% for direct student GRPO; AIME 2024 25.2 vs 19.8. Ablations: removing teacher RL costs 7.8 points, removing the forward-KL warm-up 1.7, replacing OPD with SFT-only transfer 3.3. A final student GRPO stage adds +3.1 (75.4 → 78.5). — [arXiv 2605.12483](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12483)
- The same paper generalises to Llama (Llama-3.1-8B student, Llama-3.3-70B teacher: MATH 59.8 direct GRPO, 55.4 raw-teacher bridge, 62.1 RL-teacher bridge) and code (Qwen2.5-7B ← 32B: HumanEval 87.9 → 91.2, MBPP 83.5 → 87.3 versus direct GRPO). — [arXiv 2605.12483](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12483)
- Industrial orders: SFT → OPD → RL with the claim that OPD "consistently improves downstream RL outcomes" (Muse Glimmer); experts (SFT → GRPO) → OPD replacing mixed RL (DeepSeek-V4); SFT → RLVR → iterative MOPD (Nemotron 3 Ultra); distillation last to repair sequential-RL forgetting (GLM-5). — [arXiv 2610.24628](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24628); [arXiv 2606.19348](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19348); [arXiv 2606.15007](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15007); [arXiv 2602.15763](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15763)
- Iterating the loop works: in MOPD, a second round (re-train RL teachers from the distilled student, distil again) raises the normalised score from 0.937 to 0.986 and the second-round RL teacher reaches 1.030; Nemotron's second MOPD iteration lifts Terminal-Bench 2.0 from 50.8 to 54.0, above the 50.0 teacher. — [arXiv 2606.30406](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30406v1); [arXiv 2606.15007](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15007)
- Weak-to-strong reuse of RL (Direct-OPD, ByteDance/Tsinghua, arXiv 2607.05394, 6 Jul 2026): use the log-ratio between a small post-RL model and its pre-RL reference as a dense reward on the larger target's own states; Qwen3-1.7B rises from 48.3% to 58.3% on AIME 2024 in about 4 hours on 8 A100s. **[abstract]** — [arXiv 2607.05394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05394)

**OPD + RL: mixing coefficients and gating**
- SDAR: L = L_GRPO + 0.01 · L_SDAR with gate σ(5·Δ). λ = 0.1 "causes a severe decline", λ = 0.001 gives too little signal; β = 0 (no gate) inherits OPSD's instability; in a preliminary study negative-gap tokens exceed 50% of all tokens. — [arXiv 2605.15155](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15155)
- MemOPD: L_actor = L_PPO − αH + 0.02 · L_OPD (full-vocabulary reverse KL on action tokens), λ tuned on a dev set. — [arXiv 2608.07068](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07068)
- MiMo-V2-Flash: advantage = sg[log(π_teacher / π_student)] + α · Â_ORM, α unstated. — [arXiv 2601.02780](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02780)
- OPRD: equal weights (1 : 1) on self-imitation of successful rollouts and reverse KL on failed rollouts are best; 0.25 : 1 gives 46.0 and 4 : 1 gives 46.3 against 47.9. — [arXiv 2610.21496](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21496)
- Hyperparameter-free combinations: RA-OPD's binary reward-agreement mask (+4.9 to +5.2 avg@k over OPD on three students); OPDVR's ReLU gate that makes rewards of correct trajectories non-negative and incorrect ones non-positive, reported to beat OPD on six reasoning benchmarks (abstract only, no numbers). — [arXiv 2608.27960](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27960); [arXiv 2608.24696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24696)
- verl's OPD implementation exposes this choice directly: `use_task_rewards` (default true), `distillation_loss_coef` (default 1.0), and a `use_policy_gradient` flag selecting PPO-style clipped updates versus direct GKD back-propagation. — [verl OPD docs](https://verl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algo/opd.html)

**OPD + RL: when to anneal or drop the teacher**
- ATOD: teacher weight κ 1.0 → 0.1 and reward weight ρ 1.0 → 2.0 linearly over the first 80 of 150 steps. Removing annealing is the largest ablation drop (ALFWorld/WebShop average 83.86 → 74.09). Average success for 0.6B / 1.7B / 4B students: ATOD 70.62 / 71.58 / 71.06 versus OPD 67.73 / 64.91 / 68.13 and GRPO 33.17 / 40.30 / 68.93. ATOD exceeds its teacher on ALFWorld and WebShop at all sizes; a hard OPD → GRPO switch at step 80 stays below ATOD (figure only). The authors keep κ_min > 0 as an anchor against drift. — [arXiv 2606.27814](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27814)
- RetireOPD: with a self-teacher, retiring it at step 60 gives 93.8 on ALFWorld (3B) versus 82.8 with no retirement and 75.0 for GRPO alone; linear annealing is reported as less stable and lower (no figure given). RetireOPD beats its own skill-conditioned teacher at 1.5B / 3B / 7B (89.8 / 93.8 / 95.3 versus 81.2 / 79.7 / 90.6). — [arXiv 2609.20784](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20784)
- Guided-OPD's teacher-action schedule: cosine decay of the teacher-turn probability to zero by 80% of training beats linear (36.12) and exponential (34.44) decay and any fixed β (34.41–35.85) at 37.94 average Score; higher fixed β raises Score but lowers success rate because a persistent teacher prevents the student from learning recovery. — [arXiv 2606.15912](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15912)
- OPRD: with a self-teacher, four evenly spaced teacher refreshes beat a frozen teacher; fully shared parameters are worst. — [arXiv 2610.21496](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21496)

**OPD + RL: theory**
- The survey summarises G-OPD (Yang et al., 2026; listed as arXiv 2602.12125) as proving that standard OPD equals dense KL-constrained RL, with a reward-scaling factor α > 1 (ExOPD) letting the student extrapolate beyond the teacher; multi-teacher ExOPD is reported to yield a student surpassing all same-size domain teachers. I did not read the paper. — [survey 2604.00626](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00626v4); [awesome-on-policy-distillation](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-on-policy-distillation)
- UCLA theory: forward-KL (off-policy) distillation targets an arithmetic mixture of teachers with regret ≤ 4·S·A^H·log T (tabular); reverse-KL (on-policy) targets a normalised geometric mean with regret Õ(B²H²·S·A^H·log T) under a bounded log-ratio assumption. — [arXiv 2609.38666](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38666)
- "Gains and Collapse" frames the teacher as an implicit reward model and collapse as reward hacking of that model. — [arXiv 2610.03185](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03185)
- The formula-driven survey proposes GAE-style advantages over the log-ratio reward and lists "OPD with outcome- or trajectory-level feedback" and "routed OPD theory" as open problems. — [arXiv 2606.22793](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22793)

**Teacher–student size ratio and teacher choice**
- The OPD survey's statement "When the teacher–student capacity ratio exceeds 10×, directly matching the teacher's distribution causes mode averaging" carries no citation in the text. A search-result summary of a later version (not confirmed on a page I read) says the survey calls very large gaps, such as a 400B MoE teacher into a 1B dense student, "largely unproven". — [survey 2604.00626v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00626v1); [v2 via search listing](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00626v2)
- Teacher size is a negative predictor of OPD gain across 10 teacher–student pairings (Spearman −0.61); teacher benchmark score is uninformative (−0.01); top-16 overlap is weak (+0.49); the proposed Entropy Shape Correlation computed on about 1,000 probe rollouts before training reaches +0.98 (95% CI [+0.78, +1.00]). Students 1.5–1.7B, teachers ≤ 8B, math only; the authors call it preliminary. — [arXiv 2610.39020](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39020)
- Same student, same algorithm, three teachers (ReOPD, Qwen3-4B student, math with Python tool, average of six benchmarks): OPD gives 55.1 with a 4B GRPO teacher (teacher 55.5), 51.0 with an 8B teacher (teacher 49.9) and 51.1 with a 30B-A3B teacher (teacher 62.7). The strongest, largest teacher leaves an 11.6-point gap. — [arXiv 2607.04763](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04763)
- Raw larger teachers lose to direct RL: Qwen3-1.7B on MATH gets 71.5% from a raw 8B teacher and 72.8% from a raw 14B teacher versus 75.9% for direct GRPO; the authors recommend selecting teachers by measured post-RL task reward, and among adequate teachers the smallest sufficient one. Ratios tested are about 4.6×–9×. — [arXiv 2605.12483](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12483)
- Swapping a same-origin Math teacher for the larger external Qwen3-235B-A22B hurt a Qwen3-30B-A3B student: initial per-token KL about 5× higher (~0.19 vs ~0.04), entropy fell from 0.30 to 0.21 under the policy-gradient form, and the top-k form diverged around step 18. — [MOPD, arXiv 2606.30406](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30406v1)
- For a base (not post-trained) Qwen3-1.7B student, the strongest teacher tested (Qwen3-4B-GRPO) was least effective and left the student near 2% on AIME 2025; the weaker 1.7B-GRPO teacher gave the best final accuracy. For an already post-trained 1.7B student the 4B-GRPO teacher was best. — [arXiv 2607.13399](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13399)
- A weaker same-size teacher (JustRL-1.5B) lifted an R1-Distill-1.5B student and recovered over 80% of the gap, while the higher-scoring R1-Distill-7B produced no improvement. Two conditions are proposed: compatible "thinking patterns" and capabilities the student has not already seen. Top-16 overlap rises from about 72% to above 91% in successful runs; overlap tokens carry 97–99% of probability mass. — [arXiv 2604.13016](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13016)
- Teacher noise grows with teacher size: wrong-direction advantage rate on verifiable answer tokens 30.6% (4B), 34.7% (30B-A3B), 50.6% (235B-A22B) for a small Qwen3 student. — [arXiv 2608.31046](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.31046)
- RL-trained versus SFT-trained teachers at matched task score (all Qwen3.5-9B): on the agentic domain (MobileWorld GUI tasks) the RL-teacher student scores 24.22% versus 19.94% for the SFT-teacher student, with teachers at 23.36% and 22.79%; RL teachers move 0.20–0.37× as far in parameter space as SFT teachers. — [Train4Merge, arXiv 2609.32303](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32303)
- Student-size effects in agents: with a 30B teacher, vanilla OPD collapses at 1.7B (0.17%) but not at 4B (27.64% vs teacher 30.28%); Guided-OPD's relative gains over OPD are +35.3% / +17.6% / +10.3% in Score for 0.6B / 1.7B / 4B; SAGE-OPD's tables are mixed on teacher size: under vanilla OPD a 32B teacher beats an 8B teacher for a 1.7B student on ALFWorld unseen (74.85 vs 61.94) but is slightly worse on ALFWorld seen (58.57 vs 60.00) and much worse on ScienceWorld mean score (−29.89 vs −2.78). — [TCOD](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24005); [Guided-OPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15912); [SAGE-OPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19659)
- Self-distillation and capacity: the survey reports OPSD underperforms GRPO at 1.7B (−0.5) but beats it at 4B (+0.8) and 8B (+0.9). — [survey 2604.00626](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00626v4)
- Listed industrial data point, not opened: NebulaExp-8B "finding teacher capability matters more than scale". — [awesome-on-policy-distillation](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-on-policy-distillation)

### Inferences
- A defensible default order for a 20–30B student, assembled from the above: (1) SFT cold start on teacher trajectories until the student samples the target behaviour; (2) OPD with a teacher that has itself been RL-tuned on the target environments; (3) outcome-reward RL with the teacher term annealed toward a small anchor or retired. Each step has support, but only at ≤ 9B in academic work, and the full chain is disclosed only by Muse Glimmer without numbers.
- "Size ratio" is the wrong axis in the 2026 evidence. What predicts success is distributional compatibility (shared lineage, tokenizer, prompt template, thinking mode) and whether the teacher's advantage is expressible on states the student already visits. Size correlates negatively only because larger teachers in these studies are also more distributionally distant.
- The practical implication for distilling a much larger frontier model into ~30B: expect raw white-box OPD from a very distant teacher to underperform, and consider an intermediate same-lineage teacher (RL-tuned at or near student scale), a warm-up on the teacher's distribution (Nemotron: +11.4 on GDPVal and BrowseComp), or a teacher-selection probe (ESC or top-k overlap) before committing compute. This is my synthesis; no paper tests a teacher-assistant chain for agentic OPD.
- Mixing coefficients are not portable: 0.01–0.02 applies when OPD is a gated auxiliary to GRPO/PPO with a same-size self-teacher; κ from 1.0 to 0.1 applies when OPD is the primary early signal from a stronger teacher.

### Gaps
- No controlled sweep of teacher-to-student parameter ratio for OPD on any agentic benchmark, and none at all with a student above 9B outside the MOPD ablation.
- No theory paper found on the optimal OPD-to-RL schedule; ATOD, RetireOPD and Guided-OPD are empirical and each tested on ALFWorld/WebShop-class tasks only.
- G-OPD/ExOPD, Distilled RL (2607.17247), Trust Region Policy Distillation (2607.04751), GVPO++ and KDRL were not read; their claims here come from survey or list summaries.
- No published ablation resolves "OPD instead of mixed RL" (DeepSeek-V4) versus "OPD after mixed RL" (Nemotron).

## Q6. Open-source implementations and benchmarks a researcher could start from

### Takeaway
There is a usable open stack: verl has first-class OPD (single- and multi-teacher, with task-reward mixing), EasyOPD adds a method registry on top of verl, TRL ships trainer-level distillation, and about fifteen 2026 papers released code. The gaps are a shared-tokenizer requirement in the main frameworks, no off-the-shelf OPD harness for SWE-bench / Terminal-Bench scale environments, and no standard OPD benchmark.

### Cited Findings

**Frameworks**
- **verl** — OPD documented (docs last updated 30 Oct 2026): `distillation.enabled`, per-teacher `teacher_models.<name>` with vLLM or SGLang serving, multi-teacher routing by a sample field (`teacher_key`, default `data_source`), loss modes including top-k forward KL (default top-k 32) and single-sample reverse-KL estimators, `use_policy_gradient`, `use_task_rewards`, `distillation_loss_coef`. Example scripts under `examples/on_policy_distillation_trainer/` include a multi-teacher run. Limitations stated: teacher must share the student's tokenizer and vocabulary; student-top-k reverse KL is not supported; multi-turn behaviour is not described beyond teacher log-probs being computed on the full prompt + response in the agent loop's post-processing. — [verl OPD docs](https://verl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algo/opd.html)
- **EasyOPD** (Jie Sun, Mao Zheng, Mingyang Song et al.; Shanghai Innovation Institute, Tencent, NUS), arXiv 2607.11012, v2 18 Nov 2026; Apache-2.0; built on verl 0.5.0. Implements cross-tokenizer OPD (SimCT, ULD, ALM, DSKD), on-policy self-distillation (SDPO) and step-wise OPD (SOD) with YAML configs. Its SOD reproduction: Qwen3-1.7B student with a GRPO-tuned Qwen3-4B teacher, Average@32 over AIME24 / AIME25 / LiveCodeBench-v6: base 14.14, GRPO 27.41, OPD 34.81, SOD 39.95. Black-box feedback is not implemented. — [arXiv 2607.11012](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11012); [repo](https://github.com/lds-ustc/EasyOPD)
- **thunlp/OPD** — official code for "Rethinking On-Policy Distillation" on verl 0.7.0: OPD script with five top-K token-selection strategies (default K = 16), a GRPO baseline, SFT-rollout tooling, released Qwen3-1.7B-SFT and Qwen3-4B-Base-GRPO checkpoints; its overlap diagnostics were merged into verl. About 1.1k stars. The README notes verl 0.7.0's built-in validation underestimates performance by about 5–7 points. — [repo](https://github.com/thunlp/OPD)
- **Hugging Face TRL** — GKD, GOLD (cross-tokenizer) and MiniLLM trainers, described by a curated list as the easiest starting point; EasyOPD's comparison rates TRL 0.27.0 as trainer-centric with cross-tokenizer support in experimental components. A list entry reports a TRL DistillationTrainer walkthrough with about 40× speed-up validated on Qwen3-235B → Qwen3-4B; I could not confirm that class name in documentation. — [TRL GKD trainer docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/gkd_trainer); [awesome-on-policy-distillation](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-on-policy-distillation); [EasyOPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11012)
- Other frameworks listed with OPD support (not inspected): Tinker Cookbook (off-policy KD, single- and multi-teacher OPD, multi-turn tool use recipes), slime (used by SWE-OPD, ReOPD and LGR), NeMo-RL (multi-teacher and cross-tokenizer OPD), ROLL (Alibaba), AReaL (OPD and KDRL), rLLM (agent RL with OPD over verl or tinker back-ends), SkyRL, NexRL, MS-Swift, KDFlow (arXiv 2603.01875), Trinity-RFT (used by Guided-OPD), nano-opd. — [awesome-on-policy-distillation](https://github.com/chrisliu298/awesome-on-policy-distillation); [SWE-OPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.21020); [Guided-OPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15912)

**Benchmarks and diagnostics**
- No dedicated OPD benchmark was found. The de facto agent-OPD suite is ALFWorld + WebShop + ScienceWorld (+ SearchQA), with TCOD, Guided-OPD and vanilla OPD as the usual baselines; EasyOPD states its settings "are not meant to form a single cross-regime leaderboard." — [EasyOPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11012); [OG-OPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35319)
- CAMG-II (five environments: Shop, Coding, AutoResearch, General, Visual) is a memory-focused long-horizon suite used by CAMP-II and COPD. — [arXiv 2611.14009](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14009)
- Diagnostics worth logging from day one: student–teacher top-k overlap ratio and overlap-token advantage (in verl), entropy gap, per-turn KL by depth, fraction of rollouts hitting the length limit, and the debugging check "set student = teacher, and the loss should be near zero". — [thunlp/OPD](https://github.com/thunlp/OPD); [verl OPD docs](https://verl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algo/opd.html); [Gains and Collapse](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03185)

### Inferences
- The shortest path to a working long-horizon OPD experiment is verl (or EasyOPD) with a same-family teacher, plus SWE-OPD's design choices re-implemented by hand: loss only on student-generated tokens with tool outputs masked, top-K reverse KL or sampled-token loss, an offline teacher-logit cache to cut GPU-hours by about 2.5–3.7×, and checkpoint selection by top-K overlap.
- The shared-tokenizer requirement in verl is the main practical blocker for distilling from a different model family; the options with evidence are SimCT-style alignment (EasyOPD), SWE-OPD's offline re-encoding with a sampled-token loss, or text-only methods (OLIVE, ROPD, the DAgger revisit).
- Because no shared long-horizon OPD benchmark exists and baselines are unstable across papers (Q3), any new method needs its own vanilla-OPD, SFT and RL baselines under one scaffold with at least three seeds.

### Gaps
- I did not inspect any repository's code, licence (other than EasyOPD's stated Apache-2.0) or maintenance state; listings come from paper pages and a community list.
- No public repository was confirmed for OPD on SWE-bench or Terminal-Bench environments as of 23 Nov 2026.
- The Tinker Cookbook's OPD recipes are TML material and were deliberately not examined here.
