Part one · Compute and tokens

1Summary

  • The industry sells two units. Clouds sell GPU-hours and labs sell tokens. Almost every business in between marks up one of the two, and the conversion rate between them, tokens per GPU-hour, decides most margins.
  • Renting GPUs is a spread business with financing risk. List prices for an H100 run from about $2 (GMI Cloud, DeepInfra) to about $6 per GPU-hour (CoreWeave on demand). In my model a debt-financed cluster renting at $2 needs roughly 80% utilization to break even, and the result swings by 15–20 points depending on whether GPUs are depreciated over four or six years.
  • The large neoclouds are now contract and credit businesses. CoreWeave reports $103.7B of remaining performance obligations, three customers at 72% of quarterly revenue and about $35B of debt. Its newest GPU-backed loan priced at SOFR+2.25% because an investment-grade customer anchored it, against 9–10% on its unsecured notes.
  • DeepSeek is the only lab with published serving economics. In March 2025 it showed $87,072 a day of GPU cost against $562,027 of revenue at list price. In October 2026 its V4.1-Flash deployment report claimed $38 of revenue per GPU-hour at off-peak list prices, about three times the 2025 figure, even though output prices fell from $2.19 to $0.60 per million tokens.
  • Selling data is a labor business with software ambitions. Expert-data marketplaces such as Mercor, Surge, Handshake and Micro1 book billions in gross revenue but keep only 30–40% after paying contractors. RL environment builders charge from a few hundred dollars per task to about $300k for a high-fidelity product clone, and five labs account for most of the demand.

2The value chain in one page

A paid token passes through the app, an optional router or cloud marketplace, the model lab, the cloud that rents the lab its accelerators, the chip vendor, and the foundry and memory makers. Money flows down that chain and the service flows up. Korea Investment & Securities estimates how much value each layer keeps from one dollar of token revenue.

The pattern is that scale now comes from a few anchor contracts. A neocloud signs a multi-year take-or-pay contract with a creditworthy customer, then borrows against that contract to buy the GPUs. The better the customer's credit, the cheaper the debt. That is why CoreWeave's Meta-anchored facility priced roughly seven points below its unsecured notes.

What investors and analysts argue

  • Utilization is the business. Secondary estimates put raw H100 payback near 12 months at $3/hr and full utilization, stretching to about 29 months at 40% utilization before power, networking and financing. Debt-financed clusters need roughly 70–80% utilization to cover depreciation and interest, consistent with the table above. 2nd
  • Depreciation life is the most contested assumption. CoreWeave depreciates over six years and defends it by pointing to A100 and H100 re-contracting. Michael Burry argued in November 2025 that hyperscalers' long lives understate depreciation by about $176B over 2026–28 and that the economic life is closer to two or three years. S
  • Moat and cost of capital. Kerrisdale Capital's September 2025 short report called CoreWeave a "debt-fueled GPU rental business with no moat" earning less than its cost of capital, with a $10 target. S
  • System-level demand. Sequoia's "AI's $600B Question" (2024) estimated the revenue the industry needs to justify GPU spending. Bain (2025) put the 2030 requirement at $2T a year with an $800B shortfall, and McKinsey projects $6.7T of data-center capex by 2030, $5.2T of it for AI. PS
  • Credit stress this month. Reuters reported on October 15, 2026 that CoreWeave's shares and bonds slid on debt fears, and Fortune reported a 15% one-day drop after its 2031 bond yield touched 15%. S

6How a lab turns GPU-hours into tokens

gross margin ≈ 1 − cost per GPU-hour ÷ (tokens per GPU-hour × price per token)

Tokens per GPU-hour is an engineering result. It rises with batch size, prompt caching, expert parallelism, speculative decoding, quantization and newer hardware. Price per token is a segmentation choice, with premium frontier tiers and cheap fast tiers. Cost per GPU-hour is procurement, owned or rented, contracted or spot. The DeepSeek disclosures are valuable because they publish both the throughput and the price, which lets an outsider fill in the whole formula.

What the numbers imply (my estimates)

  • At $16 per node-hour, prefill costs about $0.06 per million input tokens and decode about $0.30 per million output tokens at full load. The blended cost across all tokens is about $0.11 per million.
  • Theoretical revenue per GPU-hour is $12.9 ($562,027 ÷ 43,536 GPU-hours).
  • Priced instead at V3's lower list prices ($0.07 / $0.27 / $1.10), the same day earns about $281k, a 222% margin over GPU rent. At V3.2's September 2025 prices ($0.028 / $0.28 / $0.42) it earns about $155k, still 78% above GPU rent, before counting the efficiency gains that justified the price cut.

DeepSeek's own caveats

V3 is priced well below R1, only part of the service is monetized because the website and app are free, and night-time discounts apply. DeepSeek said actual revenue is "substantially lower" than the theoretical figure. Reuters, Bloomberg, TechCrunch and SCMP all repeated these caveats, and a Bloomberg Opinion column was headlined "DeepSeek's 'Theoretical' Profit Margins Are Just That." S

Independent checks

  • LMSYS reproduced the design on 96 rented H100s with prefill-decode disaggregation and large-scale expert parallelism. It reached 52.3k input and 22.3k output tokens per second per node on 2K-token inputs, within about 6% of DeepSeek's profile, at roughly $0.20 per million output tokens (May 2025). P
  • SemiAnalysis argued in July 2025 that DeepSeek reaches its low cost with very large batches, at the price of slow first tokens and about 20 tokens per second, and that at equal latency DeepSeek's own API is not the cheapest DeepSeek provider. Its InferenceX tool estimates R1 at $0.22–0.47 per million tokens on owned H200s, rising steeply as per-user speed increases. 2nd (paywalled, read through summaries)

8DeepSeek, October 2026

On October 20, 2026 DeepSeek open-sourced DeepInfer, the engine behind its production service, under Apache 2.0 (DeepSeek, 2026a P). The release supports V4.1-Flash on NVIDIA SM100 GPUs and Huawei Ascend 950 NPUs. It includes the kernels, model runtime, attention data parallelism, expert parallelism with load balancing and the scheduler. The KV cache system, prefill-decode disaggregation and cross-replica load balancing are not released. The README contains no prices or costs.

  • Why revenue per GPU-hour rose while prices fell. Output prices fell about 3.7x ($2.19 to $0.60) and cache-miss input prices about 3.7x ($0.55 to $0.15), but revenue per GPU-hour rose about 3x. A 96% cache hit rate means most input tokens cost almost nothing to serve, very long inputs make each request worth more, and newer hardware and kernels raise throughput.
  • How big might the cluster be. Pricing 155T tokens a day at off-peak list prices with 96% cache hits gives $1.8–3.2M a day if output is 0.5–2% of tokens. Dividing by $38 per GPU-hour implies roughly 2,000–3,500 accelerators. The output share is not disclosed, so treat this as an order of magnitude.
  • The same cautions apply. The $38 is revenue at list price, the cost side excludes training, research and staff, and Chinese press commentary on 78–86% margins (21st Century Business Herald, Caijing) rests on the same assumptions as the table above.

Part two · Selling data and environments

10Why the data and environment market exists

As pretraining on public text saturated, the labs' bottleneck moved to post-training. That needs three kinds of input that cannot be scraped. The first is human preference data for RLHF. The second is expert-written reasoning traces in fields such as law, medicine, finance and software. The third, and fastest growing, is verifiable tasks and environments for reinforcement learning on agents, meaning a sandboxed copy of real software plus a task and an automatic grader. As models improve, the work moves from cheap crowd labeling to professionals paid $50–150 an hour.

12RL environment vendors

An RL environment is a sandboxed replica of software, such as a browser, a Slack or Salesforce clone, a git repository with failing tests, or a spreadsheet or Bloomberg-terminal clone, together with tasks and an automatic grader that issues the reward. One environment can host hundreds of tasks, and labs use them for RL, evaluation and fine-tuning on successful attempts.

Risks specific to environments

  • Reward hacking. Models have found answers on the web or checked out future git commits, so graders must be adversarially robust.
  • Labs building in-house. Anthropic and xAI are hiring environment teams, and a16z partners say all large labs build environments internally.
  • Skeptics. OpenAI's Sherwin Wu said he is "short" RL environment startups.

13The business model behind selling data

  • Gross versus net. Most expert marketplaces book revenue gross and pass 60–70% to contractors, so a "$1B run rate" is often $300–400M of net revenue. That makes them closer to staffing firms than to software companies. Legacy crowd labelers ran 21–26% gross margins.
  • Customer concentration. About five labs account for most demand. Losing one is existential, as Appen found with Google, Scale with Google and OpenAI after the Meta deal, and Mercor with Meta after its breach.
  • Neutrality is a product. Labs will not send their post-training priorities to a vendor a rival owns, which is why neutral vendors gained after Meta's Scale investment.
  • Defensibility is weak but real. The assets are recruited expert networks and how fast they can be deployed, quality-control tooling, security and domain depth. Switching costs are low, and the biggest customers can build in-house.
  • Where it sits in a lab's costs. Data and environments are a second post-training cost line next to compute, and they are paid for twice, once to build each task and again in rollout compute. Mechanize's ~$2,400 of compute per task suggests compute still dwarfs data spending. My rough sum of the largest vendors' gross run rates is $6–8B in 2026, small next to lab compute budgets. U
  • Environments are the software-like exception. Reusable environments licensed to several labs could earn software margins, but the 4–5x exclusivity premium shows that labs often pay to keep them private, which cuts against reuse.

Generic "AI training dataset" market reports, such as Grand View Research's $8.6B by 2030, are far smaller than the vendors' own reported revenue, so they do not measure the frontier-lab segment and I do not rely on them.

14What to watch

  • Realized versus list revenue at DeepSeek and others. The gap between $38 per GPU-hour at list price and actual revenue is the most important unknown in token economics.
  • GPU useful life and residual value. Re-contracting rates for H100s in 2027–29 will settle the four-versus-six-year debate.
  • The refinancing wall. GPU-backed loans across CoreWeave, Nebius, Lambda and Crusoe come due in 2026–28, and October 2026 bond prices are an early signal.
  • Cache-heavy workloads. Agentic and coding workloads with long, repeated context reward whoever owns the cache. Watch whether third-party hosts close the gap.
  • Data vendor concentration. Whether labs keep buying from outside vendors or bring environment building in-house will decide if this is a durable industry or a transitional one.

AMethods and calculations

  • DeepSeek 2025 unit costs. Node-hour cost = 8 × $2 = $16. Cost per million tokens = $16 ÷ (tokens per second × 3,600 ÷ 106). Prefill at 73.7k tok/s gives $0.060, decode at 14.8k tok/s gives $0.300.
  • Revenue under other price lists. Revenue = 342B × hit price + 266B × miss price + 168B × output price, using DeepSeek's disclosed token mix.
  • Margin at $38 per GPU-hour. Margin = 1 − cost ÷ $38 for each cost assumption.
  • Implied cluster size. Daily list revenue = 155T × (1 − s) × (0.96 × $0.003 + 0.04 × $0.15) + 155T × s × $0.60, per million tokens, for output share s of 0.5–2%. GPUs = daily revenue ÷ ($38 × 24).
  • Neocloud margin grid. Per available GPU-hour, revenue = price × utilization. Depreciation = $30,000 ÷ (life × 8,760). Interest = $30,000 × 70% × 11% ÷ 8,760 = $0.26. Colocation $0.27, power $0.10, operations $0.15.
